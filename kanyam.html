Kanyam Travel Guide

Things to Do in Kanyam, Ilam

Tea rows, a working factory, horseback rides and cultural photography — here's how to spend your time at one of the best places to visit in Ilam.

Kanyam at a Glance

Why Kanyam belongs on every Ilam itinerary

Kanyam sits on a ridge in Ilam district, eastern Nepal, best known for the Kanyam Tea Estate — a working tea garden with its own factory, resident pluckers, horse rides through the rows, and some of the most photographed views in any Ilam travel guide. It's a short drive from Suryodaya Adventures, Fikkal, and the Shree Antu sunrise viewpoint.

01 · On Foot

Walk the Tea Rows

The most straightforward thing to do in Kanyam, and still the best. The rows run up and down the hillside in parallel lines, close enough together that the leaves brush your arms as you walk between them. Morning is the right time, before the mist burns off and before the day-trip jeeps arrive from Birtamode. The light between 6 and 8 AM sits low across the rows, and the mountains behind are still sharp.

During plucking season (March to May and October to November), you can watch the pluckers work. Each plucker moves along the row taking the top two leaves and a bud — the only part of the plant used for orthodox tea. The speed is what surprises most visitors: an experienced plucker covers a row in minutes.

  • Best walked 6–8 AM, before mist clears and jeeps arrive
  • Plucking season: March–May and October–November
  • Stay on marked paths to respect the working tea estate
02 · Behind the Scenes

Tea Factory Visit

The factory at the center of the estate processes the freshly plucked leaves in four stages: withering (drying the moisture out), rolling (breaking down the leaf cells), oxidation (the stage that gives tea its color and flavor), and final drying. Each stage has a different smell — the rolling room smells sharp and grassy, the drying room smells like toast.

There's no formal tour, but most staff will walk you through if you show up during working hours — just ask before going in. You can also buy freshly processed orthodox tea directly at the estate. First flush tea, harvested in March and April, is the most prized: lighter and more fragrant than later harvests. Second flush from May to June is fuller-bodied.

03 · In the Saddle

Horse Riding

Kanyam is one of the few places in Nepal where horse riding through a working tea garden is a standard tourist activity. Local operators at the estate offer rides through the rows for NPR 500 to NPR 800 per person, depending on duration. It's popular with domestic tourists and straightforward to arrange on arrival — no booking needed.

  • NPR 500–800 per person depending on duration
  • Arrange directly with operators on arrival, no booking needed
  • Rides run through the estate's own tea rows
04 · Frame the View

Photography

The Kanyam Tea Estate is one of eastern Nepal's most photogenic destinations. Its rolling green tea gardens, mountain backdrop, traditional pluckers and factory buildings create striking photo opportunities. A 20-minute walk from the factory leads to the upper tea rows, where panoramic views open across the estate — and on clear mornings, Kanchenjunga, the world's third-highest peak, rises to the north.

Kanyam is also known for cultural photography. Local shops near the main photography point rent traditional Rai, Limbu and Gurung attire along with ornaments and accessories, and professional on-site photographers are on hand to capture and print high-resolution portraits or provide digital copies against the estate's scenery.

Golden hour, 6–8 AM Kanchenjunga views on clear days Rai / Limbu / Gurung attire rental On-site photographers
